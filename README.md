# MAASA-Hackathon
The repository for team MAASA's entry for the 2026 annual NASA Hackathon

## Mission design foundation

`mission_design.py` contains the team-notes choices and typed calculation functions. Mission mass sums one choice from each spacecraft component category, the engine, and the fuel tank; required upthrust applies both the launch-site and destination modifiers. The success and progress calculations are arcade-game rules, not a physical rocket simulation. The Heavy-Duty Deep Space Pod uses the 800 kg value from the research notes.

Run the interactive game with `streamlit run app.py`. Phase 1 provides a dark interactive Plotly world map with selectable launch-site markers, a synced launch-site selector, and destination selection; confirming the location advances to Phase 2, where the spacecraft is assembled. A complete build that meets its required upthrust unlocks the Phase 3 launch sequence and returns the viewport to the top of the flight scene. Phase 3 animates a starfield journey from an Earth globe with land/ocean details to a destination-specific planetary surface; transit duration scales with orbital distance and distance, ETA, and fuel telemetry update in flight. A distinct arrival transition leads to the dedicated landing screen, which explains the controls, shows altitude, vertical velocity, fuel, and a safe-speed target bar, and offers retro-thrust/coast controls with smoothly updating telemetry during each control pulse, successful-landing scoring, or crash/restart outcomes. Landing starts at 180 m and 8 m/s, uses capped 3–6 m/s² arcade gravity, and automatically shortens control pulses near the surface to make touchdown more manageable while retaining the 5 m/s success limit. Flight, thrust, and coast have separate synthesized sound cues. Players can return to assembly from flight or landing. The sidebar offers Aurora, Solar Flare, and Arctic Pulse gradient themes, a 0–100% interface sound volume slider, Beginner Moon and Hardcore Jupiter presets, a mission reset, and a short NASA Space Apps explainer; set the volume to 0 to mute sounds. Native Streamlit dropdowns do not expose an event for merely opening the menu, so their selection cue plays when an option is chosen. Components, engine, and fuel tank start unselected and contribute 0 kg or 0 upthrust until chosen. The right-hand mission panel and upthrust progress bar remain visible throughout the mission.

Phase 2 includes a live inline-SVG spacecraft schematic that updates with component, engine, fuel tank, and theme selections. Selected-part descriptions appear alongside the drawing, with matching hover tooltips on the drawn parts; the mission readiness panel remains beside the schematic. Hull material changes the fuselage appearance, selected solar equipment adds arrays, thermal protection adds a striped insulation texture, avionics add a circuit-board detail, instruments add an optical sensor motif, and the engine and tank are drawn at the base.

Destination difficulty values are Moon 0.7, Mars 1.0, Mercury 1.2, Venus 1.7, Saturn 2.0, Neptune 1.8, Jupiter 2.1, and Uranus 1.6. Hull minimums follow the team hazard notes: Moon/Mars require Lightweight Aluminum Frame, Mercury/Venus require Carbon-Composite Shell, and Uranus/Saturn/Jupiter require Reinforced Titanium-Alloy Hull. Neptune is assigned the reinforced hull minimum as a conservative extension for the unlisted ice giant. The Hardcore Jupiter preset uses the screenshot's reinforced hull, standard solar arrays, basic sensors, passive MLI insulation, optical imaging suite, heavy-duty engine cluster, and deep-space fuel pod.
